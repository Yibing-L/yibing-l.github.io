Overview

How can a robotic furnishing translate a person’s gesture into useful physical assistance?

home+ investigates gesture-controlled assistance for people with upper-limb mobility limitations. The project connects accessible interaction with RGB-D perception, mobile manipulation, and feedback-based embedded control in a robotic furnishing designed for everyday environments.

My work integrates the software and hardware needed to move from task-level intent to robot action: robot modeling, temporal gesture recognition, target localization, collision-aware planning, and measured execution feedback. The prototype is an ongoing research platform for studying the relationship between human intent, environmental context, and physical assistance.

System overview

  1. Interpret intent

    MediaPipe hand and arm landmarks and temporal Attention-BiLSTM inference connect motion features to task-level commands.

  2. Ground and plan

    RGB-D observations, MolmoPoint/SAM2 target localization, and TF2 transforms supply 3D targets for MoveIt 2 and collision-aware planning.

  3. Execute with feedback

    Timestamped trajectories connect the host to Arduino control, with encoder feedback, protocol validation, and completion checks.

My contributions

  • Developed URDF/Xacro robot models and a ROS 2 manipulation stack using MoveIt 2, KDL inverse kinematics, and OMPL.
  • Integrated gesture recognition, multi-camera perception, and target localization with robot planning.
  • Developed closed-loop host–Arduino communication and trajectory execution with measured feedback.
  • Prototyped TSDF mapping and collision-mesh integration using nvblox.
  • Prepared study protocols, IRB materials, and experiment materials for evaluating gesture interaction under upper-limb movement constraints.
